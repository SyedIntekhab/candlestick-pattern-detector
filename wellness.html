    On the shelf

    Small, practical things. No motivational speeches.

    Resetting a session that has gone flat

    Stop the explanation. Change one thing: share a different page, switch to a blank whiteboard, or ask them to talk you through the last step instead. A short pause and one clear question restarts a session better than repeating yourself louder.

    Settling yourself before a hard lesson

    Arrive two minutes early and do nothing with your hands. Put your feet flat, unclench your jaw, and breathe out for longer than you breathe in, four or five times. Decide the first sentence you will say. Walking in with one certain thing steadies the rest.

    A breathing guide you can use while teaching

    Breathe in for four, out for six, without moving your shoulders. It is invisible from the front of a room, and the longer out-breath is what calms the nervous system. Three rounds is enough to take the edge off.

    When a class has gone badly

    Write one sentence about what actually happened, and one about what you would change. Then close the notebook. The lesson does not need to be re-run in your head all evening, and one written sentence tends to stop the loop better than thinking harder.
